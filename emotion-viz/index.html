WHAT THIS SHOWS

The upper chart traces the bankroll across 493 spins of a real playtesting session. The line is not smooth. It is a record of variance: small wins that barely offset the bet, occasional medium wins that extend the session, one dramatic spike to $185, and a long cold decline to zero.

The lower band shows the emotional state at each phase of the session. These are not hypothetical. They are reconstructed from first-person playtesting notes. The colors shift from teal curiosity through gold focus to red frustration as the session progresses and the cold variance accumulates.

The annotations mark the moments where math events produced the largest emotional shifts: the peak at $185 that anchored the player's expectations, the 48-spin dry spell that produced genuine anxiety, and the escalating $5 bets that reflected the anchoring mechanism activating.

WHAT A HISTOGRAM HIDES

A standard payout distribution histogram would show this session as an outlier: realized RTP of approximately 61% against a theoretical 90.14%. It would correctly identify that Wanted x3 hit zero times against an expectation of 5.8. It would show Meat x2 hitting at half the expected rate.

What it cannot show is that the player peaked at $185 and felt compelled to increase bets to chase $200. It cannot show that the 48-spin dry spell arrived after the peak and felt like a punishment for ambition rather than normal variance. It cannot show that the same math that produced boredom at spin 50 produced genuine dread at spin 400.

The emotional arc is not in the math. It emerges from the interaction between math events and player state. Low volatility games produce a specific arc: calibration, rhythm, anticipation, climax or resignation. This visualization makes that arc visible in a way that aggregate statistics cannot.
